## What Is Integration

Integration is the process of bringing your retreat experience into everyday life. Without it, even a profound experience can fade within a few weeks under the pressure of your usual routine.

Integration is not about “changing your life in a month”. It means slowly bringing your new understanding into each day, without forcing yourself. Noticing. Reconnecting with yourself. Taking your time before drawing conclusions.

Three signs that integration is going well:
- You notice your reactions in situations where you used to act automatically
- You allow yourself a pause before responding — both internally and in conversations
- You do not demand “results” from yourself — you simply observe

## The First 48 Hours After the Retreat

### What Is Normal to Feel

- A little uncertainty as you return to “ordinary” life
- A desire for quiet and a slower pace
- Heightened awareness — colours seem brighter, sounds louder
- Sometimes — tears or sadness (“post-retreat sadness”)
- Sometimes — euphoria and a desire to change everything right now

### Recommendations for the First Few Days

- Do not make important decisions in the first 48–72 hours
- Give yourself a break before returning to your work routine, if possible
- Write down your main insights and intentions — details fade from memory quickly

## Why Post-Retreat Sadness Happens

Returning to your usual routine after a profound experience can bring temporary sadness. This is normal — you have spent time in a different environment, and ordinary life may seem duller by comparison. It does not mean that “nothing has changed”. It means that the changes are real and need time to become part of your life.

## How to Maintain the Benefits of the Retreat

Daily practices (5–10 minutes), journaling and taking part in the group chat are enough to support the changes. You can find a detailed list of practices and a checklist for the first 30 days in the practical integration section.

The mountains will still be there. So will the inner voice you heard there.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why do I feel sad after the retreat?**
This is normal — it is “post-retreat sadness”. Returning to your usual routine after a profound experience can bring temporary sadness. It passes.

**What should I do if I feel nothing after the retreat?**
This is also a way of feeling — not a lack of results. Sometimes deeper changes become noticeable later, after a few weeks. Continue your practices without demanding immediate results from yourself.

**How can I maintain the benefits of the retreat?**
Daily practices (5–10 minutes), journaling and taking part in the group chat are enough to support the changes.

**Do I need to tell people close to me what happened at the retreat?**
Not necessarily. The experience is yours. Share what feels right, when it feels right.
